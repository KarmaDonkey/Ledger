## 1. Background

Interest rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB) is measured within the Group using both an economic value of equity (EVE) and a net interest income (NII) perspective, in line with the EBA Guidelines (EBA/GL/2022/14) and the supervisory outlier test (SOT) set out in the RTS. The current limit framework was calibrated in 2021 in a low-rate environment and has not been fundamentally reviewed since then, although minor amendments were made in 2023 relating to the treatment of non-maturing deposits (NMDs), where the behavioural modelling assumptions were updated following the back-testing exercise carried out by the 2nd line Market Risk function together with ALM.

Since 2022, the rate environment has shifted materially. The Riksbank policy rate rose from 0% to 4% and has since come down to 2.25%. Deposit betas have been higher than modelled, and the replicating portfolio for NMDs has a weighted average life of 3.4 years compared with 2.1 years at the time of calibration. As a consequence, limit utilisation for the ΔEVE under the parallel up shock has averaged 87% over the last four quarters, with two soft-limit breaches (Q2 and Q3 2026), both escalated to TC in accordance with the escalation procedure.

## 2. Analysis performed

ALM has carried out an extensive analysis of alternative calibration methodologies, including (i) a scaling of the existing limits by the change in balance sheet size, (ii) a re-calibration based on the Group's earnings-at-risk appetite derived from the ICAAP, and (iii) a hybrid approach in which the EVE limit is set as a percentage of Tier 1 capital and the NII limit is set as a percentage of projected 12-month NII. The analysis covered the six EBA shock scenarios, as well as internal scenarios including a steepener and a flattener calibrated on historical data 2000–2025. Detailed results are shown in Appendix A–D.

The hybrid approach (iii) is preferred because it aligns IRRBB limits with capital and earnings capacity, it is consistent with practice among Nordic peers and it reduces the procyclicality of limit utilisation. Under this approach the ΔEVE limit would be set at 9% of Tier 1 capital (currently approx. 6% in absolute terms) and the ΔNII limit at 5% of projected NII.

## 3. Governance

The limit framework is owned by the CFO and approved by the Board on recommendation from RCC. Market Risk (2nd line) has reviewed the methodology and has no objections in principle, but has noted that the NMD model should be re-validated before the new limits enter into force. Internal Audit has not been consulted.

## 4. Impact

The proposed limits would create headroom of approximately SEK 1.4bn in ΔEVE terms, allowing Treasury to reduce hedging costs by an estimated SEK 35m per year. Capital impact under Pillar 2 is expected to be limited.

## 5. Risks

Higher limits mean that the Group can carry more interest rate risk. Model risk relating to the NMD assumptions is considered material. There is also a risk that the supervisor views the change as a loosening of risk appetite.

## 6. Proposal

In view of the above, GAC is asked to note the analysis and to support that the proposal is submitted to GEC, RCC and the Board for approval.
